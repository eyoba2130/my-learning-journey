### 🔍 1.1 What is Research?

#### 📌 Definitions

| Perspective | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🎓 **Formal** | Advance the state of the art |
| 💬 **Informal** | Tell people something new |

#### 🧮 Ways of Explaining Research

- **Research = Analysis + Synthesis + Hypothesis**
- A combination of *work done before* and *new insights*
- **Literature survey + understanding + innovation**
- An organized and systematic way of finding answers to questions
- An academic activity involving the search for knowledge and scientific investigation
- *Re-search*: to search again, carefully and deliberately

#### 🪜 Research is Systematic

Research is systematic because it follows steps that are logical in order:

1. 🧠 Understand the nature of the problem to be studied and identify the related area of knowledge
2. 📚 Review the literature to see how others have approached or dealt with the problem
3. 🗃️ Collect data in an organized and controlled manner so as to arrive at valid decisions
4. 📊 Analyze the data in a way appropriate to the problem
5. ✅ Draw conclusions and make generalizations

> ⭐ **Two defining characteristics of research:** it is **(1) systematic** and **(2) follows a scientific method of enquiry**.

#### 🧠 Sources of Knowledge Used in Research

| Source | When it is used |
| :--- | :--- |
| 💡 **Intuitive** | Coming up with an initial idea for research |
| 📖 **Authoritative** | Reviewing professional literature |
| 🧩 **Logical** | Reasoning from findings to conclusions |
| 🧪 **Empirical** | Engaging in procedures that lead to the findings |

#### 🚫 What Research is NOT

- ❌ **Not mere information gathering.** It is not just moving facts from one place to another (fact discovery, fact transportation, fact transcription). That misses the essence of research: **the interpretation of data**.
- ❌ **Not merely rummaging for information.** The slides illustrate this with the "house for sale" signboard example.
- ❌ **Not a catchword** used to get attention.
- ❌ **Collecting and publishing existing knowledge is not research**, because it does not create new knowledge.

### 🧪 1.2 Scientific Research

#### 📌 What is Scientific Research?

- Employs **systematic observation and rational processes** to create new knowledge
- Is based on **logical relationships**, not just beliefs
- Involves an **explanation of the methods** used to collect and analyze data, plus an explanation of *why the results are meaningful*
- Is a logically stepped process used for investigating, and for acquiring or expanding our understanding

#### ⚖️ Scientific vs. Non-Scientific Research

| Aspect | 🔬 Scientific Research (SR) | 📜 Non-Scientific Research (NSR) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sources of knowledge** | Empirical evidence, experimentation, and observation | Tradition, authority, intuition, or personal experience |
| **Approach** | Logically stepped, systematic process | Relies on tradition, personal experience, intuition, logic, and authority to reach conclusions |
| **Methods** | Scientific methods and objectives | Different methods, standards of evidence, and repeatability |
| **Repeatability** | Findings can be repeated and verified | Not necessarily repeatable |

> 📝 **Note from the slides:** methods, standard of evidence, and repeatability are what differ between the two. The slides also list surveys, case studies, and qualitative interviews under non-scientific research.

### 🎯 1.3 Objectives, Motivations and Significance of Research

#### 🏹 Objectives of Research

- To **discover answers** to questions through the application of scientific procedures
- To find out the **truth which is hidden** and has not been discovered yet
- To gain familiarity with a phenomenon or achieve new insights into it
- To portray accurately the characteristics of a particular individual, situation, or group
- To determine the frequency with which something occurs or with which it is associated with something else
- To test a hypothesis of a causal relationship between variables

#### 🔥 Motivations in Research (What makes people undertake research?)

- 🎓 Desire to get a research degree along with its consequential benefits
- 🧗 Desire to solve a challenge in solving unsolved problems
- 🧭 Desire to design appropriate policies
- 📚 Desire to contribute to the existing stock of knowledge
- 😊 Desire to get intellectual joy of doing some creative work
- 🤝 Desire to be of service to society
- 🏛️ Directives of government
- 🔭 Curiosity about new things, etc.

> 💭 **Reflection question from the slides:** *What motivates you to conduct research?*

#### 🌍 Significance of Research

- Increased amount of research makes progress possible
- Research inculcates **scientific thinking** and promotes the development of **logical** habits of thinking and organization
- Complexity in business and government is increasing
- There is more information and knowledge to understand the world than before
- Multi-disciplinary professions have become common

> 💭 **Reflection question from the slides:** *What defines research's core purpose?*

#### 😅 The Joys and Frustrations of Research

| 😊 Enjoyment comes from... | 😤 Frustration comes from... |
| :--- | :--- |
| Considering a specific problem or subject field | Discovering that it is not possible to do all that was intended at the start of the investigation |

Common sources of frustration:

- The research topic may be imposed on the student
- The research must be completed within a given time period
- Funds for experiments, travel, postage, and so on may be limited or even non-existent
- The results must be presented in a specified manner
- The student may have to relate to an academic supervisor who is not interested

#### 🧭 Objectives vs. Motivations vs. Significance

| Term | Answers the Question... | Focus | Analogy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 🏹 **Objectives** | What will we do? | The specific, actionable goals of the study | The destination and stops on a road trip |
| 🔥 **Motivations** | Why are we doing it? | The personal or intellectual drive of the researcher | The reason you chose to go on the trip (adventure, family) |
| 🌟 **Significance** | So what? Who cares? | The importance and potential impact of the findings | The value and memories you bring back and share |

#### 💻 Worked Computing Example

**Sample project title:** *"Leveraging Federated Learning for Early Detection of Cardiovascular Disease using Wearable IoT Data"*

- 🔥 **Motivation:** Wearable devices generate a wealth of personal health data, but privacy concerns and data silos prevent its use for centralized AI model training. This creates a critical barrier to proactive healthcare solutions.
- 🏹 **Objective:** Design, implement, and evaluate a novel federated learning framework that accurately predicts cardiovascular disease risk from heterogeneous wearable sensor data (such as heart rate and activity) **without centralizing raw user data**, thereby preserving patient privacy.
- 🌟 **Significance:** A scalable, privacy-preserving alternative to traditional cloud-based AI in healthcare. It directly addresses ethical data handling concerns while paving the way for more accessible, personalized, and early-stage medical diagnostics.

#### 🎯 Major Purposes of Research

| Purpose | Type of Research |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🛠️ To solve a currently existing problem | **Applied** research |
| 📚 To contribute to the general body of knowledge in an area of interest | **Basic / Fundamental / Pure** research |

### 📐 1.4 Requirements and Characteristics of Research

#### 🔄 Requirements of Research

**The research process includes:**

1. Identify the problem
2. Review the literature
3. Clarify the problem
4. Define terms and concepts
5. Define the population (size)
6. Develop the instrumentation plan
7. Collect data
8. Analyze the data

**📊 Data analysis** is described as the **most important part of research**. It involves summarizing collected data and using analytical and logical reasoning to determine **patterns, relationships, or trends**.

#### 🗒️ A Research Plan Should Include

- A description of the specific investigations or experiments required to reach the set goals
- An assessment of the risks involved and possible alternatives
- A characterization of existing sources and datasets
- A description of the data collection strategy and possible alternative strategies
- An explanation of the role of each member of the research team

#### 🧑‍🔬 Researcher Qualifications

- 🎓 An undergraduate degree in a relevant field (for example psychology, sociology, economics, or health science)
- 🧪 Research experience in their industry, such as working as a research assistant
- 🌱 Intellectual curiosity, personal ambition, and a desire to serve a social need

**Research prerequisites:**

- The actuality (reality) of the research theme
- The choice of adequate research instruments and taxonomy
- The availability of the author's research capacity

#### ✨ Characteristics of Research

- 🧪 **Empirical:** based on observations and experimentation
- 🪜 **Systematic:** follows an orderly and sequential procedure
- 🎛️ **Controlled variables:** constant except for the tested or experimented data
- 💡 **Employs a hypothesis:** guides the investigation process
- 🧮 **Analytical:** data must be processed and interpreted
- ⚖️ **Objective, unbiased, and logical**
- 📈 **Employs quantitative or statistical methods, and qualitative methods**
- ✅ **Valid and verifiable**

#### 🌟 Other Traits of a Good Research Project

- An interesting or important problem
- Non-trivial (hard) challenges
- Exploration of new technology
- Can be finished in the allotted time (one semester or year)
- Effective communication (talks and reports)

#### 🔟 What True Research Is: The Eight Characteristics

> Research is a procedure by which we attempt to find, **systematically** and with the support of **demonstrable fact**, the answer to a question or the resolution of a problem.

1. 🙋 Originates with a **question**
2. 🎯 Demands a clear articulation of a **goal**
3. 🗺️ Requires a specific **plan of procedure**
4. 🧩 Usually divides the principal problem into more manageable **subproblems**
5. 💡 Is tentatively guided by **research questions or hypotheses**
6. 📌 Accepts certain critical **assumptions**
7. 📏 Will countenance only **hard, measurable data** in attempting to resolve the problem
8. 🌀 Is by its nature **circular, or more exactly, helical**

### 🧩 1.5 Types and Approaches of Research

#### 🗂️ Classification Overview

```text
Types of Research
├── Based on Purpose
│   ├── (i)  Descriptive vs. Analytical
│   └── (ii) Applied vs. Fundamental
├── Based on Approach and Method
│   ├── (iii) Quantitative vs. Qualitative vs. Mixed
│   ├── (iv)  Conceptual vs. Empirical
│   └── (v)   Behavioral vs. Design Research (IT related)
└── (vi) Other Types
    ├── Based on Time
    ├── Based on Environment
    └── Based on Orientation
```

#### (i) Descriptive vs. Analytical

| | 📋 Descriptive | 🔎 Analytical |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aim** | Accurately and systematically describe a population, situation, or phenomenon | Gather, analyze, and interpret information to reach conclusions |
| **Includes** | Surveys and fact-finding enquiries | Use of facts and information that are already available |
| **Skills needed** | Careful, systematic description | Critical thinking |
| **Best when** | Not much is known yet about the topic or problem | A deeper evaluation of existing information is needed |

> 📝 **From the slides:** your research is expected to be **more analytical**.

#### (ii) Applied vs. Fundamental

| | 🛠️ Applied | 📚 Fundamental (Basic / Pure) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aim** | Find a solution for an immediate problem facing a society or an industrial/business organization | Improve or expand the knowledge base |
| **Concerned with** | A specific problem | Generalizations and the formulation of a theory |
| **Orientation** | Solution-driven, practical, action-oriented | Knowledge-specific, theory-oriented |
| **Examples** | Developing software that automates an existing process; improving the power efficiency of mobile devices | Building and refining theory |
| **Flow** | Evaluation → Action → Social impact | |

> 📝 **From the slides:** given our field, your research is expected to be **more applied**.

#### (iii) Quantitative vs. Qualitative vs. Mixed

| Approach | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🔢 **Quantitative** | Based on the measurement of amount or quantity. Data is primarily used to find patterns and averages. |
| 💬 **Qualitative** | Concerned with quality or kind. Involves collecting and analyzing written or spoken words and texts, and studying the perception of a person, event, or product. |
| 🔀 **Mixed** | Applies both quantitative and qualitative data in the same study. Researchers validate and verify their findings by checking whether the two methods give balanced results. |

> 📝 **From the slides:** your research can follow both or either.

#### (iv) Conceptual vs. Empirical

| | 💭 Conceptual | 🧪 Empirical |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Description** | Used by philosophers to develop new concepts or reinterpret existing ones | Data-based research that can be verified by observation or experiment |

> 📝 **From the slides:** as Master's and PhD work, your research is expected to be **more empirical**.

#### (v) Behavioral vs. Design Research (IT related)

| | 🧠 Behavioral | 🏗️ Design |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Studies** | The decision processes and communication strategies within and between organisms in a social system (IT users vs. IT artifacts) | Artificial objects or phenomena designed to meet certain goals |

> 📝 **From the slides:** your work will most likely fall under **design research**.

#### (vi) Some Other Types of Research

| Basis | Types |
| :--- | :--- |
| ⏱️ **Time** | One-time research or longitudinal research |
| 🌍 **Environment** | Field-setting, laboratory, or simulation research |
| 🧭 **Orientation** | Conclusion-oriented or decision-oriented |

#### ⚠️ Expected Problems Encountered by Researchers

1. Lack of scientific training in research methodology
2. Insufficient interaction between concerned bodies
3. Research studies overlapping one another
4. Absence of a code of conduct for researchers
5. Difficulty of adequate and timely ICT know-how
6. Unsatisfactory library services and functioning
7. Difficulty of timely availability of published data
8. Ethical concerns (both animal and human)
9. Approval by authorities
10. Lack of cooperation, etc.

### 🛠️ 1.6 Research Methods and Problem Solving

#### 📌 Methods vs. Methodology

- **Research methods:** the techniques and procedures used by researchers to collect, analyze, and interpret data in order to answer research questions or test hypotheses.
- **Research methodology:** made up of research methods, the criteria for selecting those methods, and explanations of how they are used.

**Factors to consider when choosing a methodology:**

- 🎯 Research objectives, aims, and questions
- 📚 Existing literature
- 📊 Statistical requirements
- 👥 Sample size
- 🚧 Constraints

#### 🧰 Common Research Methods

| Method | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| 🔬 **Scientific method** | An empirical method based on careful observation and skepticism |
| 💬 **Qualitative research** | A popular method that lets researchers explore a phenomenon in depth |
| 🔢 **Quantitative research** | Uses questions and questionnaires to gather quantifiable data and perform statistical analysis |
| 🔀 **Mixed methods** | Combines qualitative and quantitative elements to increase understanding |
| 📝 **Surveys** | A cheap and fast method that can collect a large amount of real-time data |
| 🌍 **Ethnography** | Keeping an open mind about the groups or cultures being studied |
| 📂 **Case studies** | An in-depth examination of a single individual, group, or event |
| 🔄 **Action research** | A popular methodology in professional fields such as education, nursing, and counseling, which can incorporate a range of data collection and analysis techniques |

#### 🧱 Other Components of Research Methodology

Research design · Research instruments · Sampling · Data collection · Data analysis · Research limitations · Validity and reliability · Ethical considerations

#### 🧩 Problem Solving

- Uses existing information to **define a problem and identify solutions**
- A **research-based strategy** that addresses problems systematically
- Bases decisions on **data collected over time**

### ✍️ 1.7 Effective Report Writing Principles and Criteria for Good Research

#### 📄 What is a Research Report?

A document that **summarizes and analyzes** the findings of a research project. It is a reliable source of information that gives a **first-hand account** of the research process, data, and findings. Reports are usually written concisely and clearly.

**A typical report includes:** Abstract → Introduction → Literature Review → Methodology → Results → Discussion → Conclusion

#### ✅ An Effective Research Report Should Have

| # | Principle | Meaning |
| :-: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 🔗 **Clarity and coherence** | Understandable and logically connected. Each sentence and paragraph has one clear meaning and flows smoothly to the next. |
| 2 | ✏️ **Writing correctly** | Free from errors in grammar, spelling, and punctuation |
| 3 | ✂️ **Brevity** | The message is expressed in as few words as possible without losing essential meaning |
| 4 | ⚖️ **Objectivity** | Unbiased and fact-based, focused on evidence and analysis rather than personal feelings or opinions |
| 5 | 🎨 **Styled to the reader's taste** | Language, tone, and level of detail are adapted to the audience's background, needs, and expectations |
| 6 | 👓 **Readability** | Easy and comfortable to read, influenced by sentence structure, word choice, layout, and design |
| 7 | 🗂️ **Effective arrangement** | Information is organized logically (introduction, body, conclusion) to guide the reader through the argument |
| 8 | 🌊 **Continuity of ideas** | Smooth flow from one idea to the next using transitions and logical progression |

#### 🏅 Criteria for Good Research

- 📊 **Data analysis:** transforms raw data into a form usable for decision making
- 🧭 **Research design:** determines the approach and methodology used to answer the research question
- 🗃️ **Data collection:** can determine the success of the analysis project
- ⚖️ **Ethical:** conducted with integrity, honesty, and respect for subjects and participants
- 🎯 **Purpose clearly defined:** the purpose is clearly stated and common concepts are used
- 📢 **Findings presented unambiguously:** conclusions are justified by the data collected, with detailed findings

### 🧐 1.8 Evaluating and Reviewing Research Results

#### 📌 Why Evaluate?

Evaluating and reviewing research results is a crucial step to ensure the **validity and reliability** of findings. It means critically analyzing the **data, methodology, and conclusions** of a study to determine its strengths and limitations. The purpose of the research, the methodology, and the methods of data collection and analysis are rated for their **relevance, value, and ability to achieve the research objectives**, and to ascertain the significance of the research.

> 🔑 Factors to weigh: **sample size, research design, statistical analysis, and potential biases**.

#### 🧾 Evaluation Criteria

| Criterion | Key Questions |
| :--- | :--- |
| ✅ **Accuracy** | Is the source well documented (footnotes, citations, bibliography)? Is the information presented as fact, opinion, or propaganda? Can you verify it from referenced sources? |
| 🕒 **Currency** | Is the information current, or out of date for your topic? |
| 🔁 **Consistency** | Is the information consistent, or can you find contradictory statements? |
| ⚖️ **Reasonableness** | Is the information objective and free of emotion? Does the author give a balanced view? |
| 🎯 **Relevance** | Is the research question relevant? Does the study add anything new to current knowledge? |
| 🙈 **Bias** | Have relevant confounding factors and sources of bias been addressed? Is the language or tone biased? |
| 👥 **Peer review** | Has the study been reviewed by other experts? Do they agree? |
| 🏗️ **Design** | Are there major flaws in the design? Have the researchers identified limitations? |
| 📈 **Statistical analysis** | Were the analyses appropriate and applied correctly? Does the data support the conclusions? |

**Other factors to consider:** purpose and intended audience · authority and credibility · accuracy and reliability · objectivity or bias

### 💻 1.9 What is Research in Computing?

#### 📌 Definition

Research in computing, also known as **computer science research**, is a **systematic and creative process** of investigating, discovering, and solving complex problems related to computer technology.

#### 🔭 Areas Explored

- 🧮 Algorithms and data structures
- 🤖 Artificial intelligence and machine learning
- 🔐 Cybersecurity
- 🖥️ Computing technology and data storage systems
- 🔬 Advanced instruments and visualization environments
- 📊 Data analysis and management systems
- 🌐 High-speed computer networks
- ...and more

#### 🎯 Goal

To **advance our understanding of computing** and develop solutions that **improve our digital lives**.

#### 🧪 How It Is Done

Conducting experiments, collecting data, analyzing results, and drawing conclusions to advance the field of computing.
